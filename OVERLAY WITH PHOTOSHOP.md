# OVERLAY WITH PHOTOSHOP

In this technique, you can use a different photograph of the same subject.

1. Open the first image in Photoshop.
2. Press **Ctrl+J** to duplicate the Background layer.
3. Expand the image frame, then crop it.
4. Select the Crop Tool and set the aspect ratio to **4:5**.
5. Enable **Content-Aware**, then click **OK**.
6. Separate the subject from the background.
7. Convert the image to black and white.
8. Go to **Adjustments** and click **Black & White**.
9. With the layer mask selected, go to **Select and Mask**. You can also double-click the layer to open the mask settings.
10. In the Properties panel, set the following:
   - **Contrast:** 2
   - **Shift Edge:** -16%
   - **Amount:** 58%
   - Keep the appropriate edge-refinement option checked.
11. Select the **Refine Edge Brush Tool** and brush over the hair to remove unwanted color.
12. In the right panel, select **New Layer Mask**, then click **OK**.
13. A copy of the subject layer will remain.
14. Delete the original subject layer because it is no longer needed.

**You have now completed the first step: separating the subject from the background.**

## Apply the Overlay

1. Open the second image. This should be the background image, while the separated subject will remain in front.
2. Convert the second image to black and white.
3. Go to **Adjustments** and click **Black & White**.
4. Create a merged, visible layer by pressing **Alt+Ctrl+Shift+E**.
5. Paste the prepared overlay with **Ctrl+V**.
6. Adjust the frame by pressing **Ctrl+T**.
7. In the Layers panel, change the blend mode from **Normal** to **Soft Light**.
8. If some dark areas are distracting, add a layer mask to the selected layer.
9. Select the **Gradient Tool** and ensure the foreground color is black.
10. Open the gradient options and choose a basic **Foreground to Transparent** gradient.
11. Drag the gradient over the area you want to hide or fade.
12. If the effect is too strong or the background is too visible, reduce the overlay layer's opacity.
